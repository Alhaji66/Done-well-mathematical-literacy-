1. DNA Replication

DNA Replication is the process where a cell makes an exact copy of its DNA prior to cell division.

  • Location: Inside the nucleus during interphase.
  • Significance: Ensures genetic continuity so that every new daughter cell receives an identical set of DNA.

Step-by-Step Process:

  1. Unwinding & Unzipping: The DNA double helix unwinds. Weak hydrogen bonds between complementary base pairs break, separating the two strands.
  2. Template Exposure: Each original strand acts as a template to build a new strand.
  3. Complementary Pairing: Free DNA nucleotides in the nucleoplasm attach according to base pairing rules:
    • Adenine (A) pairs with Thymine (T)
    • Cytosine (C) pairs with Guanine (G)
  4. Backbone Joining: DNA polymerase bonds the new sugar-phosphate backbone.
  5. Semi-Conservative Result: Two identical DNA double helices are formed, each consisting of one original strand and one new strand.

2. Protein Synthesis

Protein synthesis consists of two main stages: Transcription and Translation.

Analogy: Think of DNA as a master recipe book locked inside a reference library (the nucleus). Transcription copies one recipe onto a card (mRNA) to take into the kitchen (ribosome). Translation brings ingredients (amino acids via tRNA) together to bake the cake (protein)!

Stage 1: Transcription (Nucleus)

  1. A specific section of DNA (a gene) unwinds and unzips.
  2. One strand acts as a template for making mRNA.
  3. Free RNA nucleotides pair with complementary DNA bases (Note: Uracil replaces Thymine in RNA).
  4. Single-stranded mRNA detaches, exits through a nuclear pore, and moves to a ribosome.

Stage 2: Translation (Ribosome)

  1. The mRNA attaches to a ribosome. Each 3-base sequence on mRNA is a codon.
  2. tRNA molecules in the cytoplasm carry specific amino acids and possess a 3-base anticodon.
  3. The tRNA anticodon binds to its matching mRNA codon.
  4. Adjacent amino acids are joined by peptide bonds to form a protein chain.

Key Comparisons:

Feature DNA mRNA tRNA
Strands Double helix Single linear strand Single cloverleaf strand
Sugar Deoxyribose Ribose Ribose
Bases A, T, C, G A, U, C, G A, U, C, G

Genetics & Inheritance

3. Pedigree Diagrams & Genetic Analysis

A pedigree chart tracks how a specific trait or genetic disorder is passed down through generations.

Standard Pedigree Symbols:

Unaffected Male
Affected Male
Unaffected Female
Affected Female

How to Identify Inheritance Patterns:

Pattern Key Visual Clue Genotype Rule
Autosomal Recessive (e.g. Albinism) Skips generations. Unaffected parents have an affected child. Affected individuals are homozygous recessive (aa). Parents are carriers (Aa).
Autosomal Dominant (e.g. Huntington's) Does NOT skip generations. Every affected child has an affected parent. Affected individuals have at least one dominant allele (AA or Aa).
Sex-Linked Recessive (e.g. Haemophilia) Appears mostly in males. Affected male is XhY. Carrier female is XHXh.
